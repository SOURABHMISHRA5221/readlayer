Why the RBI held the repo rate, and what the bond market heard instead

Yields barely moved after the decision. The real signal was in the governor's line on liquidity.

Before you read

This piece leans on one idea you haven't met yet. Here's the path to it.

The Reserve Bank of India kept the repo rate unchanged at 5.5% on Wednesday, as most economists had expected. Markets had priced in a pause weeks ago.

What moved traders was not the rate but the tone. The governor said the central bank would keep liquidity "adequate", a phrase bond desks read as a promise to keep buying government paper. Ten-year yields slipped two basis points before the press conference ended.

The shape of the yield curve tells the rest of the story. Short-dated bills now pay almost as much as ten-year bonds, which usually means investors expect slower growth ahead. Know term premium?

Analysts at two large banks said a cut in December is still possible if food prices keep falling, though neither expects one before the budget.
