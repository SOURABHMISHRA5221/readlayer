# M2.2 plan: open domains, glossary seeding, online concept growth, linker quality

Status: REVISED after plan-reviewer round 2 (round 1: 11 items, round 2: 8 items and 3 nits applied); round 3 pending.
Decisions: 32 (full scope), 33 (open domains, new order), 34 (targets and glossary seeding).

## Context

ReadLayer's graph has 3,327 concepts, all in four business domains (finance 1,279, economics
1,064, policy 557, business 427), built from a 290-article business corpus. Held-out linking on
15 unseen business articles is ~62% (M2.1). Decision 33 says ReadLayer isn't business-only:
signed-in readers get open domains, and demo personas stay as they are. Decision 34 fixes how this
is measured. New domains are seeded from Wikipedia glossaries. On a 30-article held-out set, each
domain gets three numbers:
1. the frozen-graph link rate, which is the headline: business ≥ 75%, non-business aim 60%, and
   under 50% means stop and tell Sourabh;
2. the after-growth link rate, labelled production behaviour;
3. grown-concept precision on a judged sample, ≥ 90%.

Outcome: a Google reader who picks only health and sports gets onboarding, calibration,
annotations and a knowledge map in those domains. A new article in any domain grows the graph
with verified concepts. The UK Chancellor, levy and Russell mislinks are fixed. The numbers above
are reported honestly.

## B. Glossary seeding (new domains, plus the business domains)

1. **Source list.** `data/glossaries.yaml` maps each Wikipedia glossary title to a domain hint.
   Every title below was probed through `safe_fetch` on 2026-10-10; the parsed entry count is in
   brackets.
   - **technology:** computer science (301), artificial intelligence (397), computer hardware
     terms (150), video game terms (750).
   - **science:** biology (397), chemistry terms (699), physics (653), astronomy (460),
     genetics and evolutionary biology (492).
   - **health:** medicine (334), diabetes (382), neuroscience (516).
   - **sports:** cricket terms (730), association football terms (≈447), American football
     (371), basketball terms (272), tennis terms (375).
   - **environment:** environmental science (352), climate change (169), ecology (345).
   - **politics:** American politics (104), international relations terms (heading format),
     European Union concepts, acronyms and jargon (heading format).
   - **law:** law (20), patent law terms (heading format, ≈207), land law (13).
   - **culture:** literary terms (762), music terminology (717), architecture (279),
     journalism (227), philosophy (346).
   - **education:** education terms A–C, D–F, G–L, M–O, P–R, S, T–Z (dl and list formats).
   - **business domains:** Glossary of economics (1,051), so the existing domains gain the
     common terms the corpus missed. The fixed held-out set is never used.
   - **Law and politics are thin.** Their final counts are reported separately.
2. **Deterministic parser** (`app/graph/glossary.py`, pure, lxml). It reads four formats:
   - `dl.glossary > dt/dd`;
   - plain `dl > dt/dd`;
   - `ul > li` that starts with a bold term followed by a dash or colon;
   - a heading per term (h3/h4) followed by its paragraph.

   Rules:
   - Navigation, "See also", references and portal boxes are skipped.
   - The term is cleaned: parentheticals become aliases, so "abstract data type (ADT)" gives
     the label "Abstract data type" and the alias "ADT". Footnote marks are stripped.
   - The definition's first sentence is kept as a context snippet only. We never store
     Wikipedia's wording as our definition: `concept_define` writes our own. Snippets never go
     into `concepts.json` or Firestore; they stay in the gitignored build cache, so no CC BY-SA
     text is served.
   - Pages fetch only through `safe_fetch` (robots honoured, 3 MB cap, 1 request/s).
   - The raw HTML is cached under `data/build_cache/glossary/` (gitignored), so reruns don't
     refetch.
   - Tests use saved fixture snippets of each of the four formats; no network.
3. **Pipeline** (`scripts/seed_glossaries.py`). It reuses `build_concepts` pieces, the build cache
   and the ledger.
   1. Parse every page to candidates `(label, aliases, snippet, domain_hint)`.
   2. Drop exact matches of existing labels or aliases. Those become alias additions to the
      existing concept only if `link_merge` says they're the same concept.
   3. Rank each domain's candidates by `wordfreq` zipf of the label, commonest first: news
      mentions common terms, not "agricultural shot". Keep the top `SEED_PER_DOMAIN`
      (default 300) for define.
   4. Dedupe among candidates and against the graph: label embeddings, cosine ≥
      `DEDUPE_THRESHOLD`, then `link_merge`, as in the build. The overrides file applies.
   5. `concept_define` v3 (§D): definition, specialization, domain from the taxonomy (the hint is
      a hint), kind, jurisdiction.
   6. Keep rule: the build's kinds plus the open-domain kinds (§D). Glossary seeds count like
      Wikipedia seeds (always kept if a kept kind), so the single-doc rule doesn't apply.
   7. `difficulty()` as built: specialization plus zipf.
   8. Prerequisites: `concept_prereqs` over a pool of the 20 nearest concepts plus the 20 most
      basic concepts *of the same domain*. At most 3, cycle breaking as built, DAG asserted.
   9. Write `data/concepts.json` locally with a new version string. `--dry-run` stops before
      that.
4. **Dry run and stop (decision 34).**
   - The dry run asserts that **no existing concept id is removed or renamed**: KNOWS edges,
     persona templates and caches use those ids (test).
   - `--dry-run` prints:
     - per domain: pages, parsed entries, candidates after exact-match drop, kept after
       ranking, merged, defined, kept by kind, final new concepts, prerequisite edges;
     - the projected `concepts.json` size and API load time (measured);
     - the LLM spend so far (the dry run does define, so spend is real but capped);
     - the exact production writes, including the corpus re-link count **with held-out docs
       excluded**, and its projected `link_confirm` cost;
     - a deterministic annotate diff (no LLM) for each demo article × both demo personas, old
       graph vs new: spans added and removed, and new spans with no cached explanation.
   - **I stop here and send Sourabh the counts and writes. Nothing is written to GCS, Firestore
     or `contents/` before his yes.**
   - Estimated spend for the dry run: ≈ 3,300 define entries in batches of 20 on `MODEL_FAST`,
     embeddings, ≈ 300 `link_merge` checks and ≈ 170 prerequisite batches, roughly $1–2. A
     `--project` cost check runs first and refuses above $4.
5. **Calibration on seeded domains.**
   - `calibration_pool` orders by `(−doc_freq, base_difficulty, id)`, so seeds (doc_freq 0) offer
     their most basic concepts first.
   - A domain is offered for calibration only with ≥ `CALIBRATION_MIN_CONCEPTS` (30).
   - Onboarding lists every domain; the comfort question still applies to any domain.

## C. Online concept growth (ingest phase 2)

1. **Where.** Inside `analyse` (`app/ingest/service.py`), after `link_demands`. Growth runs only
   for new docs from POST /v1/content, "Open here" (`user_ingested`) and, later, the extension,
   and **only when the request that created the doc came from a Google (non-anonymous) reader**
   (decision 33 scopes open domains to Google readers).
   - **Slots are reserved in phase 1**, next to `quota.reserve`, against the creating Google
     reader's `GROWTH_PER_UID_DAY` counter. `PendingDoc` stores `grow_slots: int` (no uid);
     phase 2 grows at most that many, whoever calls it. Unused slots are not refunded (simple,
     bounded).
   - A demo reader's ingest gets `grow_slots = 0`, so it never grows the graph.
   - Test: a phase 2 call by a different (anonymous) caller charges nothing to that caller and
     grows at most the stored slots.
   It never runs for held-out URLs:
   - the API ships `app/data/heldout_hashes.txt` (url hashes only);
   - a pytest asserts that file matches `data/heldout_urls.txt`;
   - the corpus scripts pass `grow=False`.
2. **Steps** (`app/graph/growth.py` for the pure parts, `app/graph/grow.py` for the I/O):
   1. The article's concept/term demands that are still unlinked, deduped by normalised label,
      capped at `GROWTH_PER_ARTICLE` (5) by salience.
   2. Embed the labels. Nearest graph concept ≥ `DEDUPE_THRESHOLD` → one batched `link_merge`.
      A "same" answer links the demand to that concept (no new concept).
   3. The rest go to **one batched `concept_define` call** with sentence context. The keep rule
      applies, with specialization ≥ 2 required (a single article).
   4. **One batched `concept_prereqs` call:** ≤ 3 from existing concepts only, then
      `validate_prereqs` and a networkx DAG check on graph + new; a cycle drops the edge.
   5. Ids from `slug_ids`, with a collision check against the graph and the growth store.
   6. Store, rebuild the graph copy-on-write, re-link the article's demands to the new concepts,
      and save the doc. The method is recorded per demand: `grown` (new), so evals can separate
      it.

   **"Verified concept"** means all of these, each with a test:
   - the output is schema-valid;
   - the domain is in the taxonomy (anything else is rejected);
   - the kind is a kept kind;
   - specialization ≥ 2;
   - dedupe passed (no merge with an existing concept);
   - prerequisites are existing ids and the graph stays acyclic.

   A fixture article with injected instructions in its text produces nothing outside the
   schema. Growth has its own timeout, **`min(20 s, time left − 5 s)`**, inside analyse, so it can't push phase 2 past the
   120 s timeout or the 150 s lease (decision 29); on timeout the article ingests without growth.
   Growth docs are written **create-if-absent**: two instances growing the same slug never
   overwrite each other; the loser re-reads and links to the winner's concept.
3. **Store.** Firestore `graph_growth/{concept_id}`: the Concept plus `added_at`, `source_cid`,
   `prompt_versions`.
   - Shared graph data, no user data; client access is denied by the existing rules (test).
   - The 256-float embedding fits easily in a doc.
   - Writing `concepts.json` from the API would need a new IAM grant (the runtime account has
     no Storage write), so it doesn't.
4. **Pick-up across instances.**
   - `app/graph/refresh.py`: at most once per `GROWTH_REFRESH_S` (60 s), a request-path
     dependency on content reads queries `graph_growth` where `added_at > last_seen`.
   - New concepts become a new graph via `set_graph` (copy-on-write, never mutated in place).
     Its version is `"<file version>+g<n>"`.
   - The content router's derived caches key on the graph version as well as the catalog
     version.
   - No LLM on this path; one small Firestore query per minute per instance.
   - Startup loads `concepts.json`, then the growth docs.
5. **Folding into `concepts.json`** (`scripts/fold_growth.py`, run by me after the live check):
   1. download the current GCS file and upload it as `concepts.prev.json` (the backup);
   2. merge the growth docs and assert a DAG;
   3. bump the version and upload;
   4. leave the growth docs in place (the startup merge skips ids already in the file).
   This meets the "new version and a backup" acceptance.
6. **Caps and cost.**
   - `GROWTH_PER_ARTICLE` 5; `GROWTH_PER_UID_DAY` 25, so one Google reader can't take the whole
     shared allowance (kept with the reader's existing daily ingest counter under
     `users/{uid}/ingest_counts`, already covered by export and delete); and `GROWTH_PER_DAY`
     100 across the service (a counter in `ops/growth/days/{day}`, transaction).
   - Every call goes through `llm.py`, so budget refusals apply. A budget refusal or cap hit
     skips growth; the article still ingests.
   - Logged per article: concepts added, merged, rejected, cost.
   - Estimated ≈ $0.004 per article (three batched `MODEL_FAST` calls plus embeddings).
   - Growth errors never fail the ingest (logged, not counted as an analyse attempt).
7. **Next library score.** The new doc is added to the catalog, and the refreshed graph and
   version bump re-score library rows (acceptance: the new concept appears in the next score).

## D. Prompts and linker quality

1. **Graph-side prompts go general**, each with a version bump and a header note. "business and
   economy news" becomes "news", with the domain scope list from the taxonomy where a domain is
   chosen:
   - `extract_demands` v→v+1 (new ingests only; stored docs are not re-extracted);
   - `concept_define` v3, `concept_prereqs`, `link_merge`, `link_confirm` v3;
   - `profile_edit` (domain list from the taxonomy).

   **Not bumped in M2.2:** `explain`, `restate`, `verify`, `verify_background`. Their versions
   are in the explanation cache keys, so a bump would turn every warmed demo explanation into a
   miss and put the M5 safety results at risk. Their "business and economy news" wording stays
   for now. Generalising them is a separate item: before/after safety evals on the frozen
   cases, plus a `warm_demo` re-run listed as a production write. It's raised with Sourabh in the
   counts message, not done here.
2. **`concept_define` v3.**
   - Domain from the 14, each with its scope line.
   - Kinds generalised: "idea" covers any field. New kept kinds: `condition` (disease or
     disorder), `method` (a technique, procedure or rule of a game), `organism_type`,
     `role` (an office or position, e.g. Chancellor).
   - New field `jurisdiction`: an ISO country code, `EU`, or `global`.
   - Build-cache entries for v2 stay valid for existing concepts; only new candidates run v3.
3. **Jurisdiction.**
   - `Concept.jurisdiction: str = "global"`.
   - Backfill for the existing 3,327 with a `concept_jurisdiction` task: batches of 50 on
     `MODEL_FAST`, label and definition only, ≈ $0.10.
   - Results go into the new local `concepts.json` with the seeding and are reviewed in the
     dry-run report (count per jurisdiction, 20 random rows).
   - `link_confirm` v3 shows each candidate's jurisdiction and the article's jurisdiction cues,
     computed deterministically in `app/graph/jurisdiction.py`: the site's country (the corpus
     source map plus TLD), currency symbols (₹, £, $, €), and country or demonym mentions in
     the paragraph. Rule: roles, institutions, laws, taxes and schemes link only within the
     same jurisdiction or to `global`.
   - **Enforced in code, not only in the prompt:** `app/graph/jurisdiction.py` drops a candidate
     whose stored `kind` is in `JURISDICTIONAL_KINDS` (role, institution, regulator, committee,
     policy, tax, scheme) and whose `jurisdiction` is neither `global` nor among the article's
     cues, before `link_confirm` sees it. The prompt also receives each candidate's kind and
     jurisdiction. Tests use stored `kind` on graph concepts.
4. **Strictness** (`link_confirm` v3 rules, with examples):
   - a specific named levy, scheme or law never links to its generic parent;
   - a paraphrase of the same concept does link.
   - **The prompt's examples never use held-out wording.** "health and care levy", "preferential
     trade access" and the Chancellor sentences come from held-out docs (Guardian Burnham,
     businessline CEPA), so the prompt uses different examples (e.g. "plastic bag levy" ↛
     "Tax levy"; "duty-free market access" → "Preferential tariffs"). The held-out sentences
     appear only as regression cases in the eval suite.
   - **Data fix:** "Tax levy" loses the alias "Tax", which made every bare "tax" an exact-match
     link to it. A new override type `remove_alias` handles this, applied by `apply_overrides`
     and the build.
5. **Missing concepts and the Russell mislink.**
   - A new override type `add` adds hand-written concepts (label, aliases, domain, jurisdiction,
     definition written by us, prerequisites from existing ids), embedded at apply time; test.
   - `add`: **Chancellor of the Exchequer** (role, GB). It gets no bare "Chancellor" alias,
     because a German chancellor would exact-match it. A UK "Chancellor" reaches it through
     embedding candidates plus `link_confirm` v3's jurisdiction rule.
   - `add`: **FTSE Russell** (index provider, global), with the alias "Russell Indexes". The frozen-graph run checks that the held-out Chancellor mention links to
     Chancellor of the Exchequer and the Russell mentions link to FTSE Russell.
   - `remove_alias` drops "FTSE Russell" and "Russell Indexes" from S&P Dow Jones Indices.
   - A new override rule, `never_merge_kind: [institution, regulator, committee, index,
     index_provider]`: two concepts both of these kinds with different labels never merge (an
     org-vs-org merge needs exact label or alias identity). Enforced in dedupe and in growth's
     merge step, against the stored `kind` of graph concepts.
   - Tests: the Russell aliases are gone after `apply_overrides`; a growth merge of two distinct
     named organisations is refused.
6. **Linker eval suite, committed before any model run (decision 26).**
   `evals/cases/link_confirm.yaml` has 14 cases:
   - Chancellor (UK, Guardian held-out sentence) with Treasury Secretary and Chancellor of the
     Exchequer as candidates;
   - "health and care levy" vs "Tax levy";
   - "preferential trade access" vs "Preferential tariffs";
   - two Russell sentences;
   - five controls that must keep linking (repo rate, yield, GST, Sensex, inflation);
   - three non-business (cricket "duck", "LBW"; medicine "hypertension").

   `evals/run.py` gains a `link_confirm` suite. Before is v2 on the current graph, after is v3.
   Both are reported in `evals/report.md`.

   - The same docs, each run through the growth steps in order against an **in-memory** copy of

5. **Number 3, grown-concept precision.**
   - A random sample (seed fixed) of 30 concepts grown in step 4, or all of them if fewer.
   - I judge each against written criteria, all four must hold:
     1. a real concept of a kept kind, not a name, event or generic word;
     2. a correct, neutral definition;
     3. a correct taxonomy domain;
     4. the demand that triggered it actually means that concept, and it doesn't duplicate an
        existing concept.
   - Each verdict and its reason is written into the report, for Sourabh to audit. Target ≥ 90%.
6. **Precision guard (BUILD_PLAN).**
   - The M2 150-mention variant sample is frozen to a file first (`evals/cases/link_variants.yaml`,
     mention ids), then scored before and after (93% before). After must not be lower.
   - Plus the hand spot-check of 10 confirmed held-out links, ≤ 2 wrong.
