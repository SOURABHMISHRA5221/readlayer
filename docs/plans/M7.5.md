# M7.5 plan: "For you" feed, "Read any link", reading goals; general explanation prompts

Status: REVISED after plan-reviewer round 2 (round 1: 16 items, round 2: 2 items and 7 nits applied) and decision 38; round 3 pending.
Decisions: 33 (M7.5 scope), 35 (growth for any reader), 37 (generalise explanation prompts),
38 (pre-seeded demo feed).
Paths: `app/`, `scripts/`, `evals/` are under `apps/api/`; `data/` is at the repo root.

## Context

M2.2 made ReadLayer open-domain: 14 domains, a seeded graph (5,206 concepts) and online growth,
live since 2026-10-10. A Google reader can now pick health and sports at onboarding, but the
library is a business corpus, and the only way in is "Open here" from a business article.

M7.5 gives readers their own way in (decision 33):
1. a **For you** feed per interest topic: articles and podcasts from Tavily, videos from YouTube,
   ranked for the reader;
2. a visible **Read any link** box on the library;
3. **reading goals**, habit and learning, shown on the profile and the knowledge map.

It also generalises the explanation prompts, which still say "business and economy news"
(decision 37).

**Demo readers (decision 38).** Each demo persona gets a predefined, pre-seeded For you feed,
served with no live search. A subtle note says the picks were prepared in advance and may be
dated, and that signing in gives a live feed. Goals stay signed-in only; demo readers see a
"Sign in to set goals" line. Read any link works for every reader within the existing caps.

## A. For you feed (API)

1. **Topics.**
   - **Google reader:** the top 6 taxonomy domains by the stored
     `users/{uid}/domains/{d}.interest`, written by onboarding and by profile edits'
     `set_domain_level`. Ties are broken by taxonomy order. With no interests stored, the four
     business domains.
   - **Demo persona:** a fixed topic pair taken from its description (`app/recs/feed.py
     PERSONA_TOPICS`):
     - newcomer: technology, economics;
     - trader: finance, business;
     - policy student: policy, politics.
2. **Search, shared and cached per (topic, format, UTC day).**
   - The only query text is the taxonomy label plus a fixed phrase:
     - article: `"<Topic> latest news"` (Tavily `topic: news`, last 7 days);
     - podcast: `"<Topic> podcast episode"` (Tavily on the podcast domains);
     - video: `"<Topic> explained"` (YouTube).
   - Never reader text, history or concept names.
   - Cache key `feed:<domain>:<format>:<YYYY-MM-DD>` in the existing `rec_cache` collection,
     shared by every reader; a stale entry is never served the next day.
   - The blocklist applies.
   - **Relevance:** the item's title or snippet names the topic label, or at least one graph
     concept of that domain, found by the readiness n-gram matcher (§A.5). Fixture test: a
     sports headline without the word "sport" passes; an off-topic one fails.
   - Articles also have to be published within 14 days when Tavily gives a date.
3. **Caps and budget.**
   - New `FEED_SEARCH_DAILY_CAP` (default 60 calls/day, all readers).
   - Feed calls also count in the existing `ops/search` counters. What protects Up next is the
     feed cap sitting below the global caps: the feed can use at most 60 of the 300 daily calls
     and at most 14 of the 90 YouTube searches. A test checks that Up next can still search once
     the feed cap is spent.
   - A Google reader with ≤ 6 topics makes ≤ 18 cache entries and ≤ 24 calls/day. The shared
     worst case is 14 topics = 42 entries and 56 calls. One Google reader who keeps changing
     interests could use the whole shared 60 in a day; that's bounded and signed-in only, and
     DESIGN §17 notes it.
   - Tavily ≈ 1 credit per search; YouTube ≈ 101 quota units per search (≤ 1,414 of 10,000/day).
   - Before the live check, STATUS §7 records Tavily's plan and remaining credits with this
     projection. If the credits wouldn't last through judging (Oct 19 – Nov 6), lower
     `FEED_SEARCH_DAILY_CAP`.
   - No LLM anywhere in the feed.
   - A cap hit serves yesterday's entry if present, else an honest empty state ("Fresh picks
     arrive tomorrow").
4. **Fetch inside the request** (Cloud Run throttles CPU after a response; decision 29).
   - GET `/v1/feed` runs the missing (topic, format) searches in parallel inside the request,
     with `asyncio.wait(..., timeout=6)`.
   - It returns what finished, with `pending: true` for the rest. Re-asks join the in-flight
     tasks (the existing `_inflight` map).
   - The client re-asks after 3 s, up to 3 times.
5. **Ranking** (`app/recs/feed_rank.py`, pure, no I/O).
   `score = interest(topic) × format_affinity(format) × readiness × goal_boost × freshness`:
   - `interest`: the stored domain interest, normalised to the reader's top topic = 1.0. Demo
     personas: 1.0 for both topics.
   - `format_affinity`: the existing per-reader model (`rank.format_affinity`, rec signals).
   - `readiness`, deterministic:
     - find the graph concepts the item names, by exact label/alias match over 1–4-word n-grams
       of its title and snippet;
     - count those whose `p_know` for this reader is below the annotation threshold;
     - k new: 1–4 → 1.0; 0 → 0.6; k > 4 → 0.8^(k−4).
   - `goal_boost`: 1.3 if the item names a learning-goal target concept (§C), else 1.0.
   - `freshness`: the existing `rank.freshness`.
   - Selection:
     - format quotas per topic: 3 articles, 2 videos, 1 podcast;
     - interleaved by score, at most 24 items;
     - each item carries a `why` line, e.g. "Health · 2 ideas new to you · helps your goal:
       Insulin resistance" (the goal's concept label, never the reader's text).
6. **Events and opening.**
   - New `Relation` value `"feed"` (`app/recs/models.py`). It is excluded from
     `relation_dismissals` and `relation_weight`, so dismissing a feed item never down-weights an
     Up next relation (test).
   - Articles open through POST `/v1/recs/open` (two-phase, ingest caps, growth slots); videos
     and podcasts open the original in a new tab.
   - Opens, shows and dismisses use POST `/v1/recs/events` with ids `feed:<hash>`.
7. **Bounded rec signals.** In `app/recs/state.py`, `seen` and `shown_counts` keep only ids that
   are still in `served` (MAX_SERVED 300). Test, and close the STATUS §5 row.
8. **Demo snapshots (decision 38).**
   - `scripts/seed_demo_feed.py` runs the same search and filter for each persona's two topics
     once.
   - It stores the raw items in `persona_feeds/{persona}` (shared, read-only to clients, no user
     data) with `prepared_at`.
   - GET `/v1/feed` for a demo reader reads that doc. No search, no caps touched. Ranking and
     `why` are computed live against the persona's state.
   - The response carries `prepared_at` and `snapshot: true`, so the web can show the note.
   - The 14-day publish filter applies only at seed time, never at serve time, so the demo feed
     can't empty itself late in judging (test with now = prepared_at + 30 days).
   - `seed_demo_feed.py --dry-run` prints the items without writing. The real run also checks
     the ≤ 12 snapshot article URLs locally (safe_fetch plus extraction, at least 400 words; no
     LLM, no Firestore writes) and drops any that fail, so a judge never hits a dead link or
     spends an ingest on one. Kept articles are not ingested by the seed.
   - A missing `persona_feeds/{persona}` doc gives the empty state ("This demo feed isn't ready.
     Sign in with Google for a live feed") with `snapshot: true`.
   - The snapshot is re-run by hand before judging (M9).
9. **Privacy.**
   - The feed reads the reader's interests, θ and knows in memory and sends nothing about the
     reader anywhere. Search calls carry only topic labels.
   - Rec signals are already in `users/{uid}/rec_signals` (export/delete covered).
   - The `persona_feeds` docs hold only public search results.

## B. Read any link

1. **The box.** A labelled field at the top of the library: "Read any link — paste an article".
   - It calls POST `/v1/recs/open` (two-phase, `user_ingested`, per-user and shared anonymous
     ingest caps, growth slots) and navigates to `/read/{id}` at once.
   - The existing error messages apply.
   - Every reader can use it (decision 35). A vitest checks the box posts the URL and nothing
     else.
2. **Pasted pages stay private to the reader who pasted them.**
   - **One shared constant**, `NOT_CORPUS = {"user_ingested", "extension"}` in
     `app/ingest/models.py`, the same name M8's approved plan uses. Docs with these sources are
     not public library content. M8 must keep this milestone's rule that the reader who brought
     a page in still sees it. This is recorded in STATUS §4 and in the AUTOPILOT_LOG note for
     resuming M8, and the M8 plan is updated when M8 resumes.
   - New user subcollection `users/{uid}/ingested/{content_id}`, `{at}`, in
     `USER_SUBCOLLECTIONS` (export and delete, test). It is written on **every successful**
     `/v1/recs/open` of a `NOT_CORPUS` doc, not only on a new URL, so a second reader who pastes
     the same link sees it too.
   - **Visibility is filtered per request, after the shared caches.**
     - The library rows cache (`_summaries`, keyed by catalog, graph, view and density) and the
       process-wide Up next library index (`_library_index`) stay shared and never include
       per-reader visibility in their keys.
     - After them, each request drops `NOT_CORPUS` docs whose id isn't in the caller's own
       `ingested` set.
     - So two judges on the same untouched persona template share the cache but never each
       other's pasted pages (test).
   - **POST `/v1/content` with a URL stays public** (source `fetch`). It isn't what the box
     uses, and the corpus and evaluation scripts ingest through it. Only `/v1/recs/open` (the
     box, "Open here", feed articles) makes private pages.
   - Reaching a doc by id is unchanged.
   - **STATUS §4 deviation row:** about 5 existing production `user_ingested` docs (4 in STATUS
     §3, plus later "Open here" opens) will disappear from other readers' libraries after the
     deploy. This is intended, not data loss; the docs stay reachable by id.

## C. Reading goals

1. **Store.** `users/{uid}/goals/{goal_id}`, a new user subcollection in `USER_SUBCOLLECTIONS`
   (export and delete, test). Google readers only; at most 5 goals. Consent is required.
2. **Habit goal** `{type: habit, metric: articles | minutes, per_week: 1–50 | 10–600}`.
   - Progress comes from this ISO week's (UTC) `weekly` doc.
   - An article counts when its annotations load in that week: `content_ids` is written by the
     annotations route.
   - Minutes = `words_read / 230`. Deterministic.
   - While learning is paused, page loads aren't recorded (decision 18), so the goal shows
     "Learning is paused, not counting" rather than silently counting nothing.
3. **Learning goal**, POST `{type: learning, text: "understand X"}` (≤ 120 chars), handled in
   `app/me/goals.py`:
   - the text is redacted (`app/privacy/redact.py`) and "understand / learn / get" prefixes are
     stripped;
   - X resolves through `resolve_names` (exact match, then embedding candidates plus
     `link_confirm`), which sees the redacted phrase only;
   - targets = the concept plus its prerequisite chain, most basic first, ≤ 8.
   - **Only the target ids and labels are stored, never the raw or redacted text.** The goal's
     display name is the main concept's label.
   - Logs carry `uid_hash`, the outcome and the cost, never the text or unresolved phrases
     (test).
   - Unresolvable → 422 `goal_not_found`, nothing stored.
   - `GOAL_RESOLVE_DAILY_CAP` 10 per reader per day.
4. **Progress math** in `app/reader/goals.py` (pure, no I/O). Learning progress = targets
   "known" / total. "Known" means band "understands" or "can explain" (p ≥ 0.5), from `knows`
   and the prior. No LLM.
5. **API.**
   - GET `/v1/me/goals` (with progress), POST `/v1/me/goals`, DELETE `/v1/me/goals/{id}`.
   - GET `/v1/me/graph?goal={id}` returns the ≤ 8 target nodes and their edges, for the map
     filter.
6. **Feed boost** as §A.5. The map marks goal targets with a ring and offers a "Goal: X" filter.
